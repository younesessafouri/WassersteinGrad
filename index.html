The paper in one sentence

Gradient explanations of weather forecasts01 can move under small perturbations02; averaging them pointwise blurs the structure03, while Wasserstein averaging preserves their spatial geometry04.

The problem

Averaging explanations pixel by pixel assumes they stay in place.

To explain a forecast, pick a target, such as the rain inside a box over Paris five autoregressive steps ahead, and differentiate it with respect to one input field, here the zonal wind at 250 hPa. The gradient G is itself a map of where in today's atmosphere the forecast is sensitive.

A single gradient of a deep network is rough; it says as much about the network's local jaggedness as about the atmosphere. The standard remedy, SmoothGrad, perturbs the input N times and averages the resulting maps, one pixel at a time:

GSG = 1N ∑i G(x + εi)

That average carries a hidden assumption about what the perturbation does to the map.

Which of the two happens inside a weather model?

The observation

Small perturbations don't blur the explanation. They move it.

Perturb the input wind once and recompute the gradient. The forecast barely changes, yet the explanation shifts. Solving the optimal-transport problem between the two maps, and masking the mass that stays where it was, shows where the rest went: out of one place and into a nearby one.

Why explanations move the architecture

Convolutions and pooling

Max pooling routes the gradient to the argmax of each window. When noise lifts a background value above the true signal, the argmax jumps and the gradient is relocated with it. Under Gaussian noise of scale s, the expected ReLU output z Φ(z/s) + s φ(z/s) stays positive even for z < 0, so inactive units fire and open gradient paths through irrelevant regions.

Self-attention

The attention matrix A multiplies the backward pass directly, so the attribution follows the token routing that A induces. Viewed as an interacting particle system, attention is non-Lipschitz with several local maxima of its interaction energy: different noise draws can land in different basins, not perturbed versions of one.

The right thing in the wrong place: meteorologists call it phase error, and forecast verification has known it for decades.

Hoffman et al. 1995 · Keil & Craig 2007 · Ebert 2008

The idea

Averaging pointwise blurs the structure. Wasserstein averaging preserves it.

Treat each explanation as a distribution of mass over the map. The pointwise mean averages how much mass each pixel holds. The Wasserstein barycenter averages where the mass is: it is the single distribution that is cheapest to reach from all of the others by moving mass.

μ∗ = arg minμ ∑i W22(μ, μi)

The Fréchet mean in Wasserstein space, where W2 is the cost of moving one distribution of mass onto another.

The method

WassersteinGrad keeps SmoothGrad's sampling and replaces its average.

  1. 01Perturb

    x + εi, εi ∼ 𝒩(0, σ2I)

    N = 20 noisy copies of one input field. The other channels stay clean.

  2. 02Gradients

    Gi = ∇x 𝒴(x + εi)

    Backpropagated through every autoregressive step to the target box. They disagree on position.

  3. 03Measures

    μi = |Gi| ⁄ ‖Gi‖1

    Each map becomes a distribution of mass over the grid, keeping positive and negative evidence alike.

  4. 04Barycenter

    μ∗λ = arg minμ ∑i W2λ(μ, μi)

    The entropic Wasserstein barycenter, solved with convolutional Sinkhorn.

  5. 05Explanation

    GW = μ∗λ

    One coherent region of influence, in the consensus position.

The one line that changes

− SmoothGrad G = 1N ∑i Gi average the values
+ WassersteinGrad μ∗ = arg minμ ∑i W2λ(μ, μi) average the geometry
Implementation details
Target
𝒴 is the output channel (surface rain) reduced over the box ℬ by a differentiable operator φ, here the spatial mean, after T autoregressive steps.
Noise
Only the selected input channel is perturbed, with σ = 0.2 × (max x − min x) and N = 20 samples.
Measure
Absolute value, then ℓ1 normalisation: it keeps the magnitude of positive and negative evidence while meeting the positivity and mass-conservation constraints of optimal transport.
Solver
An exact barycenter is prohibitive on a 512 × 640 grid. The entropic barycenter, with Sinkhorn regularisation λ = 0.001, is solved efficiently with convolutional Sinkhorn iterations.
Cost
1.96× the wall-time of SmoothGrad (14.6 s against 7.4 s per sample) on a single 16 GB laptop GPU; 1.13× at λ = 0.01.

Two readings of the barycenter

The barycenter itself is the most robust explanation, but it has no sign. Multiplying it back onto the plain gradient restores sign and sparsity.

Spatial concentration. A map spread over the whole domain is hard for a forecaster to read, however faithful.
Faithfulness · ROAD ↑
Mask the most salient pixels by linear imputation and check that the prediction degrades more than under random masks of the same size.
Robustness · LLE ↓
Local Lipschitz estimate under bounded input perturbation. LLEℓ2 measures amplitude change; LLEcos normalises the maps first, so it measures change in spatial pattern.

See it on the forecast

At t + 1, SmoothGrad leaves a speckle of opposite signs around the target; WassersteinGrad reports one region just upstream of Paris.

At t + 5 the difference sharpens. SmoothGrad fragments across Brittany with no stable organisation. WassersteinGrad still gives one coherent region, broader than at t + 1, which is the honest answer: uncertainty really is larger at longer horizons.

Methods built for photographs assume noise changes values. On physical fields, it changes positions.
